# Choroideremia

## 1. Disease Overview

Choroideremia is a rare, X-linked recessive form of hereditary retinal degeneration that affects roughly 1 in 50,000 males. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choroideremia)] [[2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6311551/)] Choroideremia is an X-linked inherited chorioretinal dystrophy leading to blindness by late adulthood. [[2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6311551/)] [[3](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/2515841418817490)] The disease causes a gradual loss of vision, starting with childhood night blindness, followed by peripheral vision loss and progressing to loss of central vision later in life. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choroideremia)] [[4](https://www.curechm.org/)]

**Key Features:** Progressive night blindness, peripheral visual field loss, and chorioretinal atrophy predominantly affecting males.

**Disease Category/Class:** X-linked inherited retinal dystrophy/chorioretinal degeneration.

## 2. Synonyms & Abbreviations

**Alternate Names:** Progressive tapetochoroidal dystrophy, X-linked choroidal dystrophy, Mauthner's disease

**Preferred Acronym(s):** CHM

## 3. Subtypes / Variants

No formal subtypes are reported for choroideremia. Although CHM patients display bilateral and symmetrical patterns of eye involvement, chromosomal disruption of Xq21 has been associated with syndromic phenotypes including CHM features with severe mental retardation and cleft lip and palate, as well as CHM phenotype with sensorineural deafness and ovarian dysgenesis. [[3](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/2515841418817490)]

## 4. Epidemiology

**Prevalence/Incidence:** The prevalence is estimated between 1/50,000-1/100,000. [[5](https://eyewiki.org/Choroideremia)] [[6](https://omim.org/entry/303100)] The prevalence of choroideremia is estimated to be 1 in 50,000 to 100,000 people. However, it is likely that this condition is underdiagnosed because of its similarities to other eye disorders. [[7](https://medlineplus.gov/genetics/condition/choroideremia/)]

**Demographics:** Since the CHM gene is located on the X chromosome, symptoms are seen almost exclusively in men. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choroideremia)] Usually, hemizygous males develop night blindness in their teenage years, followed by progressive peripheral visual field constriction and visual disability in late age. [[8](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0021515500001532)] The disease is inherited in an X-linked recessive pattern with a preponderance in the Finnish population. [[9](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/choroideremia)]

**Rarity Status:** CHM is considered a rare disease because it only affects an estimated 1 in 50,000 individuals. [[10](https://www.ophthalmologyscience.org/article/S2666-9145(23)00129-X/fulltext)] [[11](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7347087/)]

## 5. Etiology & Pathogenesis

**Primary Cause(s):** Choroideremia is caused by a loss-of-function mutation in the CHM gene which encodes Rab escort protein 1 (REP1), a protein involved in lipid modification of Rab proteins. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choroideremia)] [[12](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1471491422000521)]

**Inheritance Pattern:** Choroideremia is inherited in an X-linked recessive pattern. [[7](https://medlineplus.gov/genetics/condition/choroideremia/)] Female carriers have a 50% chance of having either an affected son or a carrier daughter, while a male with choroideremia will have all carrier daughters and unaffected sons. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choroideremia)]

**Pathophysiologic Mechanism:** The CHM gene provides instructions for producing the Rab escort protein-1 (REP-1). As an escort protein, REP-1 attaches to molecules called Rab proteins within the cell and directs them to the membranes of various cell compartments. Rab proteins are involved in the movement of proteins and organelles within cells (intracellular trafficking). [[7](https://medlineplus.gov/genetics/condition/choroideremia/)] Mutations in the CHM gene lead to an absence of REP-1 protein or the production of a REP-1 protein that cannot carry out its protein escort function. This lack of functional REP-1 prevents Rab proteins from reaching and attaching to the organelle membranes. [[7](https://medlineplus.gov/genetics/condition/choroideremia/)] Very little REP-2 protein is present in the retina, so it cannot compensate for the loss of REP-1 in this tissue. Loss of REP-1 function and subsequent misplacement of Rab proteins within the cells of the retina causes the progressive vision loss characteristic of choroideremia. [[7](https://medlineplus.gov/genetics/condition/choroideremia/)]

**Key Risk Factors:** X-linked inheritance pattern is the primary risk factor, with males being affected and females serving as carriers.

## 6. Clinical Presentation

**Core Signs & Symptoms:** The first symptom of this condition is usually an impairment of night vision (night blindness), which can occur in early childhood. [[7](https://medlineplus.gov/genetics/condition/choroideremia/)] The first symptom is generally night-blindness which occurs in childhood/young adults, followed by vision loss in the mid-periphery, which may begin when an affected individual is in their late teens or 20's. [[10](https://www.ophthalmologyscience.org/article/S2666-9145(23)00129-X/fulltext)] [[11](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7347087/)] These "blind spots" appear in an irregular ring, only leaving patches of peripheral vision, while central vision is still maintained. [[10](https://www.ophthalmologyscience.org/article/S2666-9145(23)00129-X/fulltext)] [[11](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7347087/)]

It is characterized by the early appearance of multiple scotomas in the peripheral visual field that spread and coalesce, usually sparing central vision until late in the disease. [[5](https://eyewiki.org/Choroideremia)] In choroideremia in particular, patients typically present with nyctalopia and constriction of their visual field, but visual acuity is often minimally affected in contrast to the situation in AMD. [[13](https://gene.vision/knowledge-base/choroideremia-for-doctors/)]

Additional symptoms include: Loss of acuity, depth perception, color perception, glare sensitivity and an increase in the severity of night blindness may also occur during this slow progression over the period of decades. [[10](https://www.ophthalmologyscience.org/article/S2666-9145(23)00129-X/fulltext)] [[11](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7347087/)] Other associated symptoms include photophobia, reduced contrast sensitivity and colour vision defects. [[11](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7347087/)]

**Progression Pattern:** Progression continues throughout the individual's life, but both the rate of change and the degree of visual loss are variable among those affected, even within the same family. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choroideremia)] [[4](https://www.curechm.org/)] Individuals with choroideremia tend to maintain good visual acuity into their 40s, but eventually lose all sight at some point in the 50–70 age range. [[4](https://www.curechm.org/)] [[2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6311551/)]

In male patients with choroideremia, night blindness and visual field loss begin in the first decade of life and progress until legal blindness develops much later in life, around the fifth to seventh decade. [[14](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1337/)]

**Variability between patients:** Both the rate of change and the degree of visual loss are widely variable among those affected, even within the same family. [[10](https://www.ophthalmologyscience.org/article/S2666-9145(23)00129-X/fulltext)] [[11](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7347087/)] The phenotype of choroideremia presents with high variability. [[15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19597113/)]

**Major Complications:** Affected males develop early onset night blindness, restriction of the peripheral visual field, and a decrease in central visual acuity, often leading to legal blindness in the advanced stages of the disease. [[16](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20224472/)] Additional interventions that may be needed include surgical correction of retinal detachment and cataracts, low vision services, and counseling to help cope with depression, loss of independence, and anxiety over job loss. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choroideremia)] [[4](https://www.curechm.org/)]

## 7. Diagnostic Evaluation

**Clinical Criteria:** The diagnosis of choroideremia should be suspected in males with defective dark adaptation, where poor vision in the dark is commonly the first symptom in affected males, usually as a preteen. [[17](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3020991/)]

**Laboratory Tests:** Most mutations in – and deletions of – CHM lead to absent or severely truncated REP1 protein, which can be detected by immunoblot analysis of peripheral leukocytes from affected male patients. Given that essentially all mutations in CHM lead to absent or severely truncated REP1, analysis of peripheral blood for REP1 protein expression has been used to help confirm CHM as a diagnosis and differentiate it from other diseases.

**Imaging/Instrumental Tests:** Electroretinogram (ERG) of affected males may at first show a pattern of rod-cone degeneration, which eventually becomes non-recordable. [[17](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3020991/)] Clinical evaluation of patients with choroideremia involves assessment of visual function through visual field testing and full-field electroretinogram testing and assessment of retinal and RPE structure with OCT and fundus autofluorescence (FAF). [[14](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1337/)] [[18](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK564379/)]

Characteristic fundus appearance shows patchy areas of chorioretinal degeneration generally beginning in the mid-periphery of the fundus. The areas of chorioretinal degeneration progress to marked loss of the retinal pigment epithelium and choriocapillaris with preservation of the deep choroidal vessels. [[17](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3020991/)]

**Genetic Testing:** The diagnosis of choroideremia is established in a male proband with suggestive findings and a hemizygous pathogenic variant in CHM identified by molecular genetic testing. [[17](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3020991/)] Genetic testing should be performed by an ophthalmologist specialising in genetic eye disease to confirm the diagnosis. This may be conducted with targeted retinal gene panels (~95% detection rate), Western blot analysis of leukocytes to detect the presence of REP-1 if no mutation is found on panel testing, and whole genome sequencing can be undertaken to identify mutations in intronic regions of CHM. [[11](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7347087/)]

**Formal Guidelines:** Clinically meaningful endpoints acceptable to the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and European Medicines Agency (EMA) used in CHM clinical trials include functional and structural imaging outcome measures. [[19](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0002939418305312)]

## 8. Management & Standard Therapy

**First-Line Treatments:** Currently, there is no treatment or cure for choroideremia (CHM). However, what we have is hope. [[4](https://www.curechm.org/)]

**Second-Line/Adjunctive:** While nothing currently can be done to stop or reverse the retinal degeneration, there are steps that can be taken to slow the rate of vision loss. UV-blocking sunglasses for outdoors, appropriate dietary intake of fresh fruit and leafy green vegetables, antioxidant vitamin supplements, and regular intake of dietary omega-3 very-long-chain fatty acids are all recommended. [[4](https://www.curechm.org/)] [[2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6311551/)]

**Supportive Care:** Additional interventions that may be needed include surgical correction of retinal detachment and cataracts, low vision services, and counseling to help cope with depression, loss of independence, and anxiety over job loss. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choroideremia)] [[4](https://www.curechm.org/)] [[2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6311551/)] Eye Clinic Liaison Officers (ECLOs) act as an initial point of contact for newly diagnosed patients in clinic. They provide emotional and practical support to help patients deal with their diagnosis and maintain independence. They work closely with the local council's sensory support team and are able to advise on the broad range of services provided, such as visual rehabilitation, home assessment, work and access to qualified teachers for children with visual impairment. [[11](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7347087/)]

**Preventive Measures:** For women who carry a mutation in the CHM gene, preimplantation genetic diagnosis can be used during the in-vitro fertilization process to select unaffected embryos to implant. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choroideremia)] [[12](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1471491422000521)]

## 9. Investigational / Emerging Therapies

**Therapies in Trials:** Five phase I/II clinical trials that reported subretinal injection of adeno-associated virus Rab escort protein 1 (AAV2.REP1) vector in CHM patients were included. The first-in-human gene therapy clinical trial for CHM (NCT01461213) started in 2011 using an AAV2.REP1 vector. [[19](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0002939418305312)] Overall, 40 patients were enrolled in trials, and 34 had 2 years of follow-up, with a median gain of 1.5 ± 7.2 SD (-14 min, 18 max) in ETDRS letters. [[19](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0002939418305312)] [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choroideremia)] [[20](https://www.orpha.net/consor/cgi-bin/OC_Exp.php?Expert=180)]

Unfortunately, the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) defines a clinically significant gain in visual acuity as being at least three lines, whereas two lines would have been an acceptable endpoint for the European Medicines Agency (EMA). Hence, subretinal gene therapy for choroideremia holds great potential as a therapeutic approach and the trial yielded useful clinical insights into the design and characteristics of clinical endpoints that are most appropriate for future therapeutic interventions. [[21](https://www.curechm.org/research/)]

**Trial Resources:** A number of clinical trials and natural history studies for choroideremia are currently available and listed on the National Institutes of Health's Clinical Trials website. [[22](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaophthalmology/fullarticle/2748910)] The two studies include: Efficacy and Safety of BIIB111 for the Treatment of Choroideremia (STAR) Data Contributor: Biogen Study ID: NCT03496012 and Natural History of the Progression of Choroideremia Study (NIGHT) Data Contributor: Biogen Study ID: NCT03359551. [[22](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaophthalmology/fullarticle/2748910)]

## 10. Prognosis

**Natural History:** Choroideremia is an X-linked inherited chorioretinal dystrophy leading to blindness by late adulthood. [[2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6311551/)] [[3](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/2515841418817490)] However, all individuals with this condition will develop blindness, most commonly in late adulthood. [[7](https://medlineplus.gov/genetics/condition/choroideremia/)] A study of 115 individuals with choroideremia found that 84% of patients under the age of 60 had a visual acuity of 20/40 or better, while 33% of patients over 60 years old had a visual acuity of 20/200 or worse. [[4](https://www.curechm.org/)] [[2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6311551/)]

**Impact of Therapy:** The primary endpoint, BCVA following gene therapy in CHM, showed a marginal improvement with variability between trials. [[19](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0002939418305312)] [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choroideremia)] [[20](https://www.orpha.net/consor/cgi-bin/OC_Exp.php?Expert=180)] The early results emerging from gene therapy trials for choroideremia suggest that BCVA could be improved in a proportion of patients with significantly reduced visual acuity at baseline (between 20/20 and 20/200 Snellen acuity). [[23](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33445564/)]

**Prognostic Factors:** The same study found the mean rate of visual acuity loss to be about 0.09 logMAR per 5 years, which is roughly 1 row on a Snellen chart. [[4](https://www.curechm.org/)] [[2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6311551/)] Foveal function is relatively preserved until the fifth decade of life. [[24](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11605660/)] Disease progression variability exists even within families, and female carriers may experience mild symptoms later in life due to X-inactivation patterns.
